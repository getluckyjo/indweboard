**Subject:** The app economics — how the insured swing actually works

Hi Brendon,

Thanks for the read-through, and for pushing on this one. You're right that those numbers need context — on their own they invite exactly the wrong question.

Here it is in plain terms.

**The golfer pays per swing, not a subscription for unlimited swings.**

Every insured swing is bought on the tee. The golfer picks their stake before they hit: $1 for a shot at $500, up to $100 for a shot at $100,000. One entry buys one swing at one par 3.

That distinction is the whole thing. Premium is collected on the same swing that creates the risk. A subscription buying unlimited swings would have premium fixed and exposure unbounded — no underwriter would take it, and rightly so.

**The $10/month is a membership, not a per-swing price.**

It gives the golfer the app plus one insured swing a month at the $10,000 tier. Anything beyond that they buy on the tee like everyone else. I've reworded that line in the deck, because "$10/month, one insured swing" was reading as $10 a swing — my fault.

**Where the premium goes.**

24% of every entry is ceded to the underwriter as premium, in exchange for 100% cover of every verified ace. Of that premium, 20% is paid to Indwe as brokerage.

So Indwe isn't taking a slice of the golfer's stake. Indwe earns commission on premium, the same as on any placed risk.

**Why the risk is attractive.** Per entry, on the modelled mix:

- Premium: $4.08
- Expected claim: $1.30
- Loss ratio: 31.9%
- Combined ratio after brokerage: 51.9%

At amateur odds of one ace in 12,500 attempts, aces would have to occur **2.51 times more often than the odds predict** before the cover stops making money. Largest single exposure is capped at $100,000. And every swing is GPS-verified inside the insured hole, with a registered ball and a tee cam, before it counts as anything.

Worth adding: stake size is display-driven, not skill-driven. The $100 entry is bought by the player who wants an audience, not the player most likely to hole out — so the biggest prizes don't attract the best golfers.

**The three-year numbers on that slide.**

1.91 million insured swings. R143.96m of premium ceded. R28.79m of that back to Indwe as brokerage — R1.44m in year one, R8.01m in year two, R19.34m in year three.
